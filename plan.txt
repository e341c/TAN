--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0. ONE-PARAGRAPH SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The trend model shows no measurable edge on data it has not seen. That statement
is weaker than "it is worse than a coin flip" and the difference matters: the
holdout AUC point estimate is 0.4794, but with 389 heavily-overlapping labels the
effective sample is ~76 independent observations, and the confidence interval
comfortably contains 0.5. We cannot currently tell a broken model from a working
one at this sample size. Before any modelling work, three things are wrong that
have nothing to do with features or architecture: the deployed weights were fitted
on data ending 2025-05-02 (16 months stale), the deployed run is the argmax of 12
logged runs ranked by a test-side metric, and the reported confidence intervals
treat overlapping labels as independent. Fix the measurement and the deployment
protocol first (Tier 0-1); they are cheap, they are provably wrong today, and
every later experiment is unreadable without them.

1.5 The confidence intervals are too narrow — labels overlap heavily
    mean average_uniqueness over the holdout = 0.195
    -> 389 resolved anchors carry roughly 76 independent observations.
    median span = 6 bars, mean 6.9 (max_holding = 24)

    apps/backtest.py:442 and core/metrics.bootstrap_ci resample anchors i.i.d.
    With uniqueness 0.195 the true CI is roughly sqrt(1/0.195) = 2.3x wider than
    reported. Every accuracy CI printed anywhere in this repo for a triple-barrier
    model is overconfident by about that factor.

    Note also: median span 6 means the "24h barrier" label typically resolves in
    ~7 hours. The config comment describing it as a day-long aggregate is wrong
    in practice, and the mapping to a trade (a 24h hold) does not match the label.

1.6 The model is badly calibrated against the current base rate
    mean p_up = 0.481   actual UP rate = 0.612   calibration gap = +0.131
    p_up range = 0.326 .. 0.656 (median 0.481) — not collapsed, but narrow.

    Reliability by quintile on the holdout:
      p in (0.325,0.415]  n=78  mean p=0.384  actual UP=0.692
      p in (0.415,0.468]  n=78  mean p=0.445  actual UP=0.615
      p in (0.468,0.498]  n=77  mean p=0.482  actual UP=0.545
      p in (0.498,0.527]  n=78  mean p=0.510  actual UP=0.551
      p in (0.527,0.656]  n=78  mean p=0.583  actual UP=0.654

    The curve is U-shaped, not monotone: the least-confident-UP bucket has the
    HIGHEST realized UP rate. That non-monotonicity is what pushes AUC below 0.5.
    At n=78 per bucket this is not conclusive on its own, but it is consistent
    with a model whose operating point has drifted rather than one that ranks well.

    class_weight: auto rebalances to the TRAINING prior, which was ~50/50. The

1.8 Feature drift is real but moderate
    Mean shift of each feature in the holdout vs the frozen scaler's fitted
    distribution, in training standard deviations:
      mtf_1d_rsi_14          +1.42
      mtf_1d_ret_14          +1.30
      mtf_1d_dist_vwap_14    +0.96
      range_per_vol_168      +0.82
      mtf_4h_rsi_14          +0.78
      amihud_168             +0.60
      mtf_4h_ret_14          +0.59
    5 of 52 features shift more than 0.75 sd. None reach the +-4 clip.
    No dead (constant-in-training) features in this run.

    Verdict: drift contributes but does not by itself explain the result. The
    daily-timeframe momentum block is the drifting part, which is expected — a
    14-day RSI in a strong uptrend is genuinely elevated.

1.9 The barrier is anchored on a price the model cannot see
    features.add_features pairs window X[i-seq_len:i] (bars up to i-1) with
    labels[i], and triple_barrier_labels anchors the barriers at close[i] and
    scans from i+1. So the model predicts a race between close[i]*exp(+2.5s)
    and close[i]*exp(-2.5s) without observing close[i].

    Measured on the holdout window:
      1-bar log-return sd            0.00499
      barrier half-width (2.5*sigma) 0.01136
      unobserved move / barrier      43.9%

    Nearly half the barrier width is consumed by a single bar the model never
    sees. At serving time this is worse than untidy: the last closed bar is T,
    so the window corresponds to i-1 = T, and the barrier belongs to close[T+1],
    a bar that has not happened. Any consumer placing real stops has to
    substitute the current price, which is a different level than the label used.

1.10 Sample weighting is extremely concentrated
    raw_w = |realized| * average_uniqueness, normalised to mean 1:
      median 0.625   max 23.06   top 10% of samples carry 39.1% of total weight
    Effective training sample size is far below the nominal 22389 rows.

1.11 The GBM baseline is NOT degenerate (a hypothesis I checked and rejected)
    train_runs_v2.csv shows auc_gb = 0.49999966 for the last three runs, which
    looks like a constant predictor. It is not: refitting fold 2 gives
    AUC 0.4871 with p in 0.241..0.806 and 3852 distinct values. The 0.4999 in the
    log is the 3-fold MEAN, which happens to land near 0.5. The real finding is
    duller: GBM on the last bar of the window is at or below chance on fold 2,
    so blending it 50/50 (train_v2.py:175) drags the ensemble down
    (auc_blend 0.5638 < auc_ens 0.5855). The blend should not be a headline.

2. ROOT CAUSES, RANKED BY EXPECTED IMPACT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. We cannot measure a 0.53-AUC edge with the data we test on. (Section 3.1)
   Power analysis, using the measured bootstrap SE of 0.029 at n=389 and
   correcting for uniqueness 0.195:
     to distinguish AUC 0.53 from 0.5:  ~1400 independent labels = ~7200 anchors = ~10 months
     to distinguish AUC 0.55 from 0.5:  ~ 500 independent labels = ~2600 anchors = ~3.5 months
     to distinguish AUC 0.58 from 0.5:  ~ 200 independent labels = ~1000 anchors = ~1.4 months
   A three-week holdout can only detect an edge far larger than any this model
   has ever claimed. Until the measurement is fixed, every experiment below is
   a coin flip dressed as a result.

B. Deployment ships a stale, unselected artifact. (1.3, 1.4)
   Sixteen months of unseen regime, plus run-level selection on a test metric.
   These are protocol bugs with known fixes and no research risk.

C. The target does not match either the model's information set or the trade. (1.5, 1.9)
   Barrier anchored on an unobserved bar; median resolution 6 bars against a
   24-bar nominal horizon; label overlap of 5:1 that nothing downstream accounts for.

D. No calibration or prior adaptation. (1.6, 1.7)
   The decision threshold is hardcoded at 0.5 and the training prior is frozen.

E. Feature set is momentum-heavy and drifts; no order-flow or positioning data. (1.8, Tier 3)
   52 features, all derived from OHLCV + 2 crypto benchmarks + 2 daily macro
   series. Nothing about the order book, liquidations, basis, or realised flow.

F. Architecture is probably not the bottleneck.
   Listed last deliberately. The 184-run log showed zero hyperparameter
   correlation, seed std 0.005, and GBM within noise of the LSTM. Changing the
   model class before fixing A-D will produce another unreadable number.

3. TIER 0 — MEASUREMENT (do this first; nothing else is interpretable without it)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 Block-bootstrap confidence intervals that respect label overlap
    Problem: core/metrics.bootstrap_ci resamples i.i.d.; triple-barrier labels
    have uniqueness 0.195.
    Change: add `block_bootstrap_ci(values, spans, n_boot=4000)` to
    core/metrics.py that resamples contiguous blocks of length >= max_holding,
    or weights the resample by uniqueness. Use it in apps/backtest.py:442,
    apps/holdout_eval.py, and train_v2.run_fold.
    Also print effective sample size (sum of average_uniqueness) next to every n.
    Acceptance: the holdout accuracy CI widens from 43.2-53.2% to roughly
    38-58%; the report states n=389 (n_eff~76).
    Effort: 0.5 day. Risk: none. Value: every future comparison becomes honest.

3.2 A purged, embargoed rolling-origin backtest instead of one holdout window
    Problem: the current holdout is 3 weeks. Section A shows that is 10x too
    short to resolve the edge being claimed.
    Change: add `apps/rolling_eval.py`. For each month M from 2024-01 to now,
    train on [start, M - embargo], evaluate on M. Report the distribution of
    monthly AUC/accuracy, not a single number. This is expensive (about 30
    training runs) but it is the only way to see whether an edge is stable
    across regimes or is one lucky quarter.
    Cheaper interim version: keep the model fixed and evaluate the DEPLOYED
    artifact month by month over its true out-of-sample period (2026-01 onward,
    from fold 2's test window plus the holdout). That needs no retraining and
    gives ~8 monthly points immediately.
    Acceptance: a table of monthly AUC with n_eff per month, and a
    pooled estimate with a block-bootstrap CI.
    Effort: 1 day for the fixed-model version, 3 days for the retraining version.

3.3 Make the run log record validation metrics, and stop selecting on test
    Problem: train_runs_v2.csv records auc_ens/auc_gb/auc_blend/acc_ens — all
    TEST-side. There is no val column, so nobody can select correctly even if
    they want to (1.4).
    Change:
      - add auc_val, val_loss, n_eff_test to the CSV row in train_v2.py:308
      - add a `scripts/select_run.py` that calls core.validation.select_by_val
        over the log and prints which run SHOULD be deployed
      - print expected_max_of_noise over the log's auc_val on every run, so the
        operator sees when their best run is inside the noise band
    Acceptance: rerunning selection on the existing 12 runs names a run by
    auc_val; if that run differs from 2026-08-17T15-47-17, redeploy it and note
    the change in the README.
    Effort: 0.5 day.

3.4 Freeze a permanent, never-trained-on tail
    Problem: today's holdout moves forward every time you refetch, and any model
    retrained after 2026-09-07 will have trained on part of it.
    Change: reserve the last N months as a locked evaluation set, recorded by
    date in assembly/config.yaml, and make train_v2.py refuse to read rows past
    that date unless an explicit --use-holdout flag is given. data_fetch/holdout.py
    already computes the export-stamp cutoff; extend it with a configured
    `frozen_eval_start`.
    Acceptance: training aborts with a clear error if the config's data_path
    contains rows after frozen_eval_start.
    Effort: 0.5 day.

3.5 Record a data cutoff inside meta.json
    Today the only durable record of what a model saw is its export stamp
    (which is what data_fetch/holdout.py uses, deliberately). Add the actual
    max(ts) of the training span, the fold index, the split boundaries and the
    row count to meta.json at export time (core/export_onnx.export_model).
    Acceptance: meta.json for a new run contains train_end_ts, val_end_ts,
    test_end_ts, fold_id, n_rows.
    Effort: 0.25 day.

4. TIER 1 — PROTOCOL AND DEPLOYMENT FIXES (cheap, provably wrong today)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 Refit the deployed model on all available data  [HIGHEST EXPECTED VALUE]
    Problem: 1.3 — shipped weights end 2025-05-02.
    Change: after walk-forward evaluation selects a CONFIGURATION on val, do a
    final refit on the full series (minus the frozen eval tail from 3.4) using
    the epoch count / early-stopping epoch distribution learned from the folds,
    and export THAT model. The walk-forward run's job is to estimate performance
    and pick hyperparameters; it should not also be the source of the artifact.
    Implementation sketch in train_v2.main():
      folds  = [run_fold(...) for each split]            # estimate + select
      n_ep   = int(np.median([f["best_epoch"] for f in folds]))
      final  = train ensemble on (0, frozen_eval_start) for exactly n_ep epochs
      export(final)                                       # not folds[-1]
    Caveat to state honestly in the code: the final model has no held-out
    estimate of its own. Its expected performance is the walk-forward mean, and
    that must be what meta.json reports, clearly labelled.
    Acceptance: meta.json train_end_ts is within one week of the data's max ts.
    Effort: 1 day. Expected impact: large but unquantified — this is the single
    biggest difference between the artifact and what the protocol intends.

4.2 Anchor the barriers on the last OBSERVED close
    Problem: 1.9 — 43.9% of the barrier width is an unobserved bar.
    Change: in features.add_features, either
      (a) pair window X[i-seq_len:i] with a label whose barriers are anchored at
          close[i-1] and whose path scan starts at bar i; or
      (b) keep the anchor at close[i] but include bar i in the window (i.e. pair
          X[i-seq_len+1:i+1] with labels[i]).
    (b) is a one-line index change and makes the label answer "from the price I
    can see right now, which barrier is hit first" — which is also what the
    trading bot needs. (a) is more invasive but keeps the current window.
    IMPORTANT: this changes the anchoring convention that apps/backtest.py,
    models/trend/predict.py and apps/test_backtest.py all encode. Update all
    four together, and `test_batch_scoring_matches_the_live_path` will catch a
    partial change.
    Acceptance: the live path and the backtest still agree to 1e-3; the barrier
    the bot places equals the barrier the label used.
    Effort: 1 day including the consumer updates.
    Expected impact: moderate. Removing 44% of irreducible noise from the target
    definition should show up in val AUC if anything will.

4.3 Recalibrate the output, and stop hardcoding 0.5
    Problem: 1.6 — calibration gap +0.131; 1.7 — prior shift 50/50 -> 62/38.
    Change:
      - fit isotonic regression or Platt scaling on the VALIDATION fold and
        store the calibrator in the scaler bundle next to target_spec
      - apply it in models/trend/predict.run_trend_onnx (or bake it into the
        ONNX graph as the quantile model does with its conformal offsets —
        models/quantile/model.py:71 is the pattern to copy)
      - replace the hardcoded `p_up >= 0.5` in apps/backtest.py:309 and in the
        bot with a threshold chosen on validation
      - report Brier score and a reliability table alongside AUC everywhere
    Note: calibration fixes the DECISION, not the RANKING. It will improve
    accuracy and the -13.11pp edge against majority; it will not move AUC. Say
    so in the report so nobody reads a calibration fix as a discovery.
    Acceptance: holdout Brier score improves; mean p_up tracks the realised base
    rate within the CI; reliability table is monotone.
    Effort: 1 day.

4.4 Adaptive class prior
    Problem: class_weight: auto freezes the training prior.
    Change: either drop class weighting entirely and let calibration handle the
    prior (cleaner), or re-estimate the prior on a trailing window at serving
    time and shift the logit by log(pi_new/(1-pi_new)) - log(pi_train/(1-pi_train)).
    The second is a standard prior-shift correction and is two lines.
    Risk: a trailing-window prior is itself a momentum bet. Test it on the
    rolling evaluation from 3.2 before shipping — it may simply be buying the
    recent trend, which is a strategy, not a model improvement.
    Effort: 0.5 day + evaluation.

4.5 Stop reporting the 50/50 blend as a headline
    Problem: 1.11 — GBM is at chance on fold 2, so the blend is strictly worse
    than the ensemble on the exported fold.
    Change: keep the GBM as a diagnostic baseline (it is a good one), but choose
    the blend weight on validation rather than fixing it at 0.5, and allow 0.
    Effort: 0.25 day.

4.6 Cap the sample weights
    Problem: 1.10 — top 10% of samples carry 39.1% of the weight.
    Change: winsorise raw_w at, say, the 95th percentile, or use
    sqrt(|realized|) * uniqueness instead of the raw product. Sweep the cap on
    validation.
    Effort: 0.25 day + a sweep.

4.7 Turn off continuous_training as a deployment path
    config.yaml:7 sets continuous_training: true. Combined with the run log
    being test-side (1.4), this is a machine for generating noise-max artifacts.
    Continuous training is fine as a search process; it must not be allowed to
    pick what ships. Gate deployment behind 3.3's select_run.py.
    Effort: 0.25 day.

5. TIER 2 — TARGET REDESIGN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Ordered by (expected value / effort). Each is a separate experiment, each judged
on validation only, each reported with n_eff and a block-bootstrap CI.

5.1 Fix the horizon/label mismatch
    Median span is 6 bars against max_holding 24 (1.5). Two coherent options:
      (a) reduce max_holding to ~8 and widen the barrier so the label resolves
          near the vertical barrier rather than long before it; or
      (b) keep 24 bars and widen the barriers (3.5-4.0 sigma) so the horizontal
          barriers stop dominating.
    (b) was tried at 3.5 sigma and lost on val (0.5122 vs 0.5237 at 2.5), but
    that sweep was done with the current, mis-anchored label (4.2) and the
    stale-fold artifact. It is worth redoing once 4.2 lands.
    Sweep on VAL only: upper/lower in {1.75, 2.5, 3.5}, max_holding in {6, 12, 24, 48}.
    That is a 12-cell grid; note in advance that with 12 cells the expected max
    of noise is ~1.6 sd above the mean, so require the winner to beat the second
    place by more than the seed std (0.005) before believing it.

5.2 Meta-labelling (Lopez de Prado)  [most promising target change]
    Instead of asking the LSTM for direction, take a simple primary signal
    (e.g. MTF trend filter, or the sign of a moving-average cross) and train the
    model to answer "should I take this signal or skip it?" — a binary bet-sizing
    problem on a much cleaner label. This converts a hard direction problem into
    an easier precision problem, and it directly produces the abstention the
    trading bot wants.
    Why it fits here: the model already has a usable top-decile signal in
    training (0.5980 vs 0.5060 majority on fold 2). Meta-labelling makes that
    the entire objective instead of a footnote.
    Effort: 3-5 days. Evaluate as precision at fixed coverage, not AUC.

5.3 Three-class target with an explicit abstain class
    Currently unresolved anchors (-1) are dropped from training AND from
    accuracy. On the holdout that is 17.4% of anchors — a sixth of the data
    silently discarded. Predicting "neither barrier" as a third class uses them
    and gives the bot a reason not to trade.
    Effort: 2 days.

5.4 Regress the realised move instead of classifying its sign
    triple_barrier_labels already returns `realized`. Training on it (Huber loss,
    volatility-normalised) uses magnitude information that the binary label
    throws away, and the sign of the prediction recovers the classifier.
    Effort: 2 days.

5.5 Predict volatility or barrier-touch TIME rather than direction
    An honest fallback if direction stays unmeasurable. "Will the barrier be
    touched within N bars" and "how wide should the barrier be" are both easier
    problems, both useful to the bot, and the quantile model already shows this
    family works here (holdout coverage 91.09% vs 90% nominal — the one thing in
    this repo that demonstrably generalises out of sample).
    Effort: 3 days. Strategic value: high — it is where the measured edge is.

6. TIER 3 — FEATURES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Current set: 52 features from OHLCV + ETH/SOL + DXY/VIX (config.yaml:134-157).
All price/volume-derived. The gaps below are ordered by expected information gain
per unit of engineering effort.

6.1 Order-flow and microstructure (highest value, moderate effort)
    Binance provides these and the fetcher already talks to the API:
      - taker buy/sell volume imbalance per bar (aggTrades, or the klines
        taker_buy_base / taker_buy_quote columns, which binance_klines.py
        ALREADY FETCHES at lines 52-53 and then DROPS at line 57)
      - trade count vs volume (average trade size is present; add its skew)
      - order-book imbalance at top N levels (depth snapshots, needs a collector)
      - realised spread / effective spread proxies
    The taker-buy column is free: it is already in the response, already
    discarded. Adding taker_buy_base/volume as a feature is a one-line change to
    binance_klines.fetch_klines plus a feature in build_features_df. Do this first.
    Expected: order-flow imbalance is the single most-cited short-horizon
    predictor in the microstructure literature. If nothing here helps, that is
    itself strong evidence about the ceiling.

6.2 Derivatives positioning (high value, low effort — partly present)
    funding_rate is already fetched and used (2 features: funding_rate_scaled,
    funding_rate_cum3). Missing:
      - open interest (fapi /futures/data/openInterestHist) and its rate of change
      - long/short account ratio and top-trader position ratio
      - basis: perp price vs spot index, and the term structure vs quarterly
      - liquidation volume (forced flow is mechanically predictive of short-term
        reversals)
    Effort: 2 days for the fetchers + freshness checks (extend
    data_fetch/binance_klines.check_freshness, which already exists for this).

6.3 Cross-sectional / market-structure
      - BTC dominance and USDT dominance (config.yaml:144-147 has btc_d and
        usdt_d wired with empty paths — the plumbing exists, only the CSVs are
        missing)
      - breadth: fraction of top-N alts above their own 24h/7d moving average
      - a proper alt index rather than SOL alone (alt_index is also wired, empty)
    Effort: 1-2 days. Note that this closes three placeholders already in the config.

6.4 Volatility term structure and regime
      - realised vol at 24/72/168 exists; add the RATIO structure explicitly
        (rv_24/rv_168) as a regime indicator
      - Parkinson / Garman-Klass / Rogers-Satchell estimators from OHLC — much
        lower variance than close-to-close at the same sample size
      - a regime label (trending/ranging via ADX or a Hurst exponent) either as a
        feature or as a gate that selects between two models
    Effort: 1 day. Low risk, likely small gain.

6.5 Time and session structure
      hour_sin/cos and dow_sin/cos exist. Missing:
      - explicit Asia/Europe/US session flags and session-open/close proximity
      - CME futures gap indicator and weekend flag (BTC trades through the
        weekend; the derivatives complex does not)
      - proximity to funding settlement (8h cycle) — mechanically important
      - macro release calendar (CPI, FOMC) as a blackout/volatility flag
    Effort: 1-2 days. Funding-cycle proximity is the highest-value item here.

6.6 On-chain (highest effort, most speculative)
      exchange net flows, stablecoin supply changes, miner flows, active
      addresses. Requires a paid data source and careful lag handling (most
      on-chain aggregates publish with delay; using them at their nominal
      timestamp is a leak). Defer until 6.1-6.5 are exhausted.

6.7 Feature pruning — probably as valuable as adding
    52 features on ~4500 effective training samples (22389 rows x uniqueness
    ~0.2) is a poor ratio. Run:
      - permutation importance on validation, per fold
      - clustered feature importance (group correlated features before ranking;
        with 3 correlation windows and 3 amihud windows the set has obvious
        collinear blocks)
      - drop everything below noise and re-run; a 15-feature model that matches
        a 52-feature model is a better model, and it will drift less (1.8 shows
        the drifting block is the mtf daily group)
    Effort: 2 days. This may be the single highest-value item in Tier 3.

6.8 Feature-drift gating in production
    core/validation.feature_drift already exists and train_v2 already calls it.
    Wire it into serving: compute the drift of the live window against the
    scaler's fitted distribution, and refuse to emit a signal (or widen
    uncertainty) when more than K features exceed the threshold. On today's
    holdout that would have flagged 5 features at >0.75 sd.
    Effort: 1 day.

7. TIER 4 — ARCHITECTURE (last, deliberately)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The 184-run log established that hyperparameters do not matter on this data
(hidden_size correlation +0.000, seed std 0.005). model.py:9-12 already argues
for a Transformer. That may be right eventually, but it is the wrong next move:
a new architecture evaluated with the current measurement apparatus (section 2A)
will produce a number nobody can interpret.

Order, once Tier 0-1 is done:

7.1 A linear/logistic baseline on the same features
    If a regularised logistic regression on window aggregates matches the LSTM,
    the sequence model is not earning its keep. This is a 1-hour experiment that
    should have existed from the start and it bounds every later claim.

7.2 Gradient boosting on engineered window aggregates
    Not GBM on the last bar (which is what train_v2.py:169 does and which sits at
    chance). Give the trees min/max/mean/slope/last of each feature over the
    window. Trees handle 52 correlated features on a small effective sample much
    better than an LSTM, and this is the strongest realistic contender.
    Effort: 2 days.

7.3 Temporal CNN / dilated convolutions
    Cheaper to train than attention, better inductive bias for fixed-length
    windows than an LSTM. Effort: 2 days.

7.4 Transformer encoder / TFT
    Only if 7.1-7.3 show that sequence structure is being under-used and the
    measurement from Tier 0 can resolve the difference. With n_eff ~ 4500
    training labels, a Transformer is very likely to overfit.
    Effort: 1-2 weeks.

7.5 Ensembling across ARCHITECTURES rather than seeds
    Seed ensembling already removed the seed lottery. Averaging structurally
    different models (logistic + trees + LSTM) usually helps more, and their
    disagreement is a free uncertainty estimate (core/validation.ensemble_spread
    already computes it and nothing consumes it).
    Effort: 1 day once 7.1-7.2 exist.

8.1 Scheduled retraining with a fixed cadence and an automatic staleness alarm.

11. KILL CRITERIA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This section exists because the repo's own history (184 runs plateauing at 0.53,
then 12 more plateauing at 0.52 +- 0.02) suggests the answer may be that there is
no edge to find at 1H on OHLCV-derived features. Decide in advance what would
settle it.

  - If after Phase 2 the correctly-built, correctly-measured artifact has a
    pooled out-of-sample AUC whose block-bootstrap CI contains 0.5 across >= 6
    monthly windows, stop adding features and go to 5.5 / 6.1. Direction at 1H
    is not the product.
  - If Phase 3's order-flow features do not move validation AUC by more than the
    seed std (0.005), conclude that OHLCV-derived information is exhausted and
    that further gains need a different data class, not a different model.
  - If meta-labelling (5.2) cannot reach precision materially above the base
    rate at any coverage level, the direction signal is not tradable regardless
    of how it is packaged.
  - Positive kill criterion, stated so success is also falsifiable: the trend
    model is worth deploying when, on the frozen evaluation tail, it beats the
    majority baseline by a margin whose block-bootstrap CI excludes zero across
    at least 6 non-overlapping monthly windows.

Meanwhile the quantile model's interval coverage (91.09% out of sample against a
90% nominal) is the one thing in this repo that demonstrably generalises. If the
trend work stalls, that is where the product is.

16. EXECUTION LOG — A SPAN-ALIGNMENT BUG, AND WHAT IT INVALIDATED (2026-09-08)
================================================================================

16.1 The bug

  `build_span` is asked for the bars in [start, end) and hands back windows. It
  trimmed its warm-up lead-in BY COUNT — drop `len(X) - (end - start)` rows from
  the left — which is only correct if `add_features` returns one row per bar.

  It does not. Two filters sit in between:
    * build_features_df ends with a global dropna().reset_index(drop=True);
    * the windowing loop skips anchors whose label is unresolved, whose label
      resolves past the end of the frame, or whose window still holds a NaN.

  Together they drop ~51% of candidate bars on a monthly span, non-contiguously.
  So len(X) came out BELOW the bar count, the trim computed zero, and nothing was
  trimmed at all. Consequences, all silent:

    a) every val/test span began ~200 bars before its own start, inside the split
       it was supposed to be held out from;
    b) rolling_eval's per-bar prior was read at `arange(end-len(X), end)` — an
       index that ran +47 bars AHEAD of the true anchor on average (max +189);
    c) warm-up of seq_len+200 was in any case too short for a 168-bar feature
       set plus the MTF block, so each span silently lost its first 186 bars.

  Nothing failed. Shapes were right and metrics were plausible.

16.2 The fix

  features.add_features(return_index=True)  reports the anchor bar of every row
                                            it kept, mapped back through
                                            build_features_df's re-indexing by
                                            timestamp.
  train_v2.build_span                       trims with an exact mask on those
                                            positions, and can return them.
  train_v2.span_warmup()                    one definition, seq_len+400, replacing
                                            seq_len+200 repeated at five sites.
                                            400 saturates (measured).
  rolling_eval                              uses the true anchor bars for the
                                            prior, and persists them per row.
  models/trend/test_spans.py                5 tests. The load-bearing one asserts
                                            labels[reported_bar] == returned y;
                                            3 of the 5 fail against the old code.
  rolling_eval --label-autocorr on|off      so an A/B is a flag, not a config edit.

16.7 The lesson this repo keeps re-learning

  Section 2A says every experiment is unreadable until the measurement is fixed.

  like an array of bars and was an array of rows. The pattern is the same each
  time — a quantity that is plausible, unchecked, and load-bearing.

  The countermeasure that worked here was not review, it was an assertion that
  ties two representations together: labels[reported_bar] == returned_y. Prefer
  that shape of test over any amount of commentary.

17. IS IT TRADEABLE? (2026-09-08)  — apps/tradeability.py
================================================================================

The question "the AUC is 0.55, can it be traded" has a different answer from
"does the ranking carry information", and this repo had no tool that answered
the first. apps/tradeability.py does, from the rolling-eval JSON, using the real
realised outcome behind every scored anchor (the `bar` index added in 16.2 is
what makes that join possible; labels[bar] == y at exactly 1.0000, which is also
an independent check on the alignment fix).

Payoff model: take-profit at the barrier the label used, stop at its mirror —
literally the trade the label describes. Half-width ±2.5σ ≈ 1.137%, median hold
6 bars. Optimistic about fills, so read the gross figures as an upper bound.

17.1 THE RESULT THAT DECIDES IT

  Sequential (one position at a time — what one account can do), 8 bp round trip:

    coverage  trades    acc   gross/trade  break-even   net/trade     t      p
        2%      123   60.16%    +0.2718%     27.2 bp     +0.1918%   2.42  0.015
        5%      240   55.83%    +0.1712%     17.1 bp     +0.0912%   2.07  0.039
       10%      480   52.92%    +0.0753%      7.5 bp     -0.0047%   1.32  0.186
       20%      972   50.93%    +0.0164%      1.6 bp     -0.0636%   0.41  0.683
      100%     3718   50.78%    +0.0073%      0.7 bp     -0.0727%   0.37  0.709

  Break-even at 10% coverage is 7.5 bp against a realistic 8-10 bp taker cost.
  The strategy sits ON the cost line, not above it. Block CI on net per trade at
  10% coverage: [-0.1150%, +0.1065%] over 480 trades — straddles zero.

17.2 THE TRAP THAT MAKES IT LOOK GOOD

  Scoring every anchor as its own trade gives a completely different picture:
  +0.1050%/trade net at 10% coverage, t = 6.94, p = 0.000, total +241.78%.

  That is not a strategy, it is unlimited capital. Mean uniqueness is 0.186, so
  the 2303 anchors in the top decile are ~430 independent bets; the overlapping
  view takes up to two dozen simultaneous positions in whatever direction the
  model currently leans. Its entire return comes from three trending months
  (+241.39%, i.e. 100% of the total; the median month is exactly 0.000% and only
  12/36 are profitable). Under the one-position constraint the same configuration
  returns -2.25%.

  Any backtest of an overlapping-label model that does not enforce a position
  constraint will produce this illusion. It is the economic twin of the
  n-vs-n_eff error the whole plan has been correcting.

17.3 WHERE IT IS NOT YET FALSIFIED

  At 2% coverage (123 trades in three years) the edge clears costs: 60.16%
  accuracy, break-even 27.2 bp, p = 0.015. Reasons not to believe it yet: the
  coverage level was chosen by reading this table (7 comparisons), only 10/36
  months are profitable, and 123 trades is a small sample. It is a hypothesis to
  test prospectively, not a result.

17.4 AGAINST SECTION 11'S OWN CRITERION

  "The trend model is worth deploying when, on the frozen evaluation tail, it
  beats the majority baseline by a margin whose block-bootstrap CI excludes zero
  across at least 6 non-overlapping monthly windows."

  Not met, and not close: mean edge vs majority is -5.07 pp with 11/36 months
  positive. The ranking edge (AUC 0.5512, 28/36 months, p = 0.0006) is real and
  is NOT the same thing as a tradeable edge.

  Verdict: do not trade this with real money. The honest statement is "not shown
  to make money", which is weaker than "shown to lose money" — at 8 bp the
  sequential CI contains both signs.

17.5 What would change the answer

  * A target built for the trade rather than adapted to it (5.5: touch-time and
    volatility are where this repo's only out-of-sample-validated skill lives).
  * Meta-labelling (5.2), which optimises precision at low coverage directly —
    17.3 says that is the only region with anything in it.
  * Costs and the position constraint inside the objective, not bolted on after.
  * Prospective paper trading before capital, since every number above is a
    backtest of a procedure that has never run forward.

  Something is there and it is the same something as everywhere else. Both
  meta-AUCs land at ~0.52, which is where the direction model, three feature
  sets and four barrier geometries all landed. The meta-label does not make the
  problem easier; it re-expresses the same thin signal.

20.2 A LABEL BUG, CAUGHT BY THE SAME CHECK AS LAST TIME

  The obvious test for "a barrier was touched" is `spans < max_holding`. It is
  wrong at a frame's right edge: `triple_barrier_labels` clips its scan to
  `min(i + max_holding, n - 1)`, so an anchor with nowhere left to look also
  reports a short span and reads as touched. The `label_end >= n_rows` guard
  does not catch it, because the clipped span puts label_end just inside.

  Caught by asking the same question that validated the alignment fix — can the
  label be reconstructed from the frame? `touched[bar] == y` was False. With the
  last `max_holding` anchors excluded it is True, and
  test_resolution_label_matches_the_frame now pins it.

  Third time this exact check has earned its keep (16.2, 18.3, here). It is the
  cheapest reliable tool in the repo.

20.3 IT DOES NOT FIX THE ECONOMICS

  Joined to the direction model by bar, over the same 36 origins, priced per
  ENTERED trade at 8 bp:

    no filter                       n=25416   resolve 88.75%   net -0.0515%
    resolution top 10%              n= 2542   resolve 99.25%   net -0.0268%
    direction top 10%               n= 2249   resolve  100%*   net +0.0591%
    res top 50% AND dir top 10%     n= 1284                    net +0.0325%
    res top 30% AND dir top 10%     n=  799                    net +0.0132%

    * the direction run only ever contained resolved anchors, so that 100% is
      the optimistic resolved-only view flagged in 18.3, not a real filter.

  Adding the resolution filter to the direction filter makes it WORSE at every
  setting. Two reasons, both structural:

    * it selects volatile stretches, and direction is no easier there — on
      resolution-filtered anchors direction accuracy is 50.1-52.7%, i.e. nothing;
    * at 2.5 sigma the base resolve rate is already 88.75%, so lifting it to
      99.25% moves the direction accuracy you need from 53.91% to only 53.50%.

  The arithmetic is the whole story: net = P(resolve) x (2*acc_dir - 1) x width
  - cost. Resolution improves the first term. Direction is the second, it is
  ~0.52, and no amount of the first fixes a second that sits at zero.

  Where the leverage WOULD be real is the wide geometry: at 5.0 sigma / 12 bars,
  filtering to the top decile takes the required direction accuracy from 55.16%
  down to 52.64%. But 18.4 measured direction at the wide geometry on this same
  harness and it collapsed to ~50%. Both halves have to work; only one does.

20.4 What this actually establishes

  A genuine, large, reproducible predictive capability — on a quantity that is
  not directional. That is worth stating plainly rather than filing as another
  negative:

    * It is the first thing here to clear the noise floor by a wide margin and
      hold in every single month.
    * Its natural uses are non-directional — volatility/straddle exposure, or
      sizing and trade timing — and this bot is directional, so realising it
      needs a product decision, not another model.
    * It corroborates the quantile model rather than competing with it: both say
      the forecastable object in 1H BTC is volatility. Two independent methods
      now agree on that, and five methods agree that direction is not.

20.5 Leak tests (models/trend/test_resolution.py)

  An AUC of 0.805 where everything else is 0.52 is either the first real finding
  here or a leak, so it was attacked rather than accepted. Four tests:

    test_label_ignores_candles_beyond_the_holding_window
    test_unobservable_anchors_are_excluded
    test_feature_window_ignores_future_candles
    test_permuted_labels_destroy_the_signal      <- the load-bearing one

  THE CONTROL: train the identical pipeline on the identical windows with the
  training labels permuted. Real 0.8607, permuted 0.4446. The signal comes from
  the data, not the harness.

  Both tests were then checked by INJECTING the faults they claim to catch, and
  the first attempt found them too weak:

    * The permuted bound was one-sided (`< 0.60`). Feeding the outcome in as a
      feature and permuting made the model learn the inverse — 0.1179, which is
      "below 0.60" while proving the pipeline hands over the answer. A clean
      pipeline must score CHANCE on permuted labels, so the bound is now
      two-sided: |permuted - 0.5| < 0.10. It catches the injection.
    * Restoring the clipped-scan bug in features.py fails
      test_unobservable_anchors_are_excluded and, independently,
      test_resolution_label_matches_the_frame in test_spans.

  A structural argument backs the tests up: the resolution target shares its
  feature pipeline, windowing loop and walk-forward split with the direction
  target. Any lookahead in that shared path would inflate both. Direction stays
  at 0.52-0.55 on identical inputs, so a leak would have to live in the handful
  of lines that differ — which is what these tests cover.

20.6 Next

  1. Decide what to DO with a volatility signal. Straddle/gamma exposure and
     vol-scaled position sizing are the honest candidates; both are product
     questions before they are modelling ones.
  2. If direction work continues at all, it needs a different data class
     (section 11's kill criterion): order book, liquidations, basis — not
     another target or feature built from OHLCV.
  3. Still outstanding: feature-importance re-run under the fixed harness
     (16.8 item 1).

21.3 A LIVE BUG FOUND ON THE WAY IN

  The scheduler mounted the data directory READ-ONLY:

    ../technical_analysis_trainer/data:/app/.../data:ro

  but macro_fred — already in that crontab, daily — writes data/train and
  data/predict. As configured it fails on write every day, into a log nobody
  reads. That is exactly the failure its own crontab comment describes: "a stale
  file does not error ... this is exactly how two of the 54 features went dead in
  production". The cron job added to fix that could never have worked, and the
  prod overlay only swaps images so it inherits the same mount.

  Fixed by dropping `:ro` for the scheduler only; the api service still mounts
  it read-only, which is correct — it only reads.

  Worth noticing that this was found by asking "where does this write, and can
  it?" rather than by any test. Nothing in the suite covers deployment wiring.

21.4 A CORRECTION I HAD TO MAKE TO MY OWN REASONING

  merge_history() was written to replace `concat` + `groupby("ts").last()`, on
  the stated grounds that the latter takes the newest row wholesale and would let
  a NaN from a failed endpoint erase a stored column.

  That was wrong. GroupBy.last() skips NaN by default, so the stored value
  survives on its own; the suite passes against both implementations. The claim
  was checked only after being written into the docstring as justification.

  The explicit version is kept, but for an honest reason rather than the invented
  one: the NaN-skipping is an easily-missed default, and the neighbouring idioms
  do not share it — swapping in `.nth(-1)` or `.tail(1)` DOES erase the column,
  and two of the six tests catch that. They guard a future edit; they are not
  evidence of a past fix, and the docstring now says so.

21.5 Where this leaves the direction question

  Until then the honest position is unchanged from 19.5 and 20.4: direction is
  not tradeable on OHLCV, volatility is strongly predictable, and the product
  decision about what to do with a volatility signal does not depend on this
  collector at all. This is a bet placed now so that it can be settled later.

23. LIQUIDATION COLLECTOR (2026-09-10) — and a failure mode I built and then hit
================================================================================

The second half of 21: forced flow. A liquidation is not a trade anyone chose to
make, it is the exchange closing a position at market, so it is mechanically
price-moving and mechanically clustered — the most-cited short-horizon predictor
this repo does not have.

23.1 Why it is a daemon

  /fapi/v1/allForceOrders is 404. The only source is the websocket stream, which
  remembers nothing. binance_derivatives can recover from a 29-DAY outage; this
  cannot recover from a 29-SECOND one.

  Shipped as its own compose service (`liquidations`, restart: unless-stopped)
  rather than a cron entry, with the data directory mounted read-write.

23.2 ZERO IS DATA — and the first design got it wrong

  A calm hour has no liquidations. A dead collector also has no liquidations.
  If those look alike the eventual feature is "forced flow, except when the
  process was down, where it is confidently zero". So the collector writes a
  heartbeat on a fixed interval and coverage is computed from heartbeats, not
  from events: heartbeat + no events = a real zero; no heartbeat = a hole.

  THEN THE HEARTBEAT ITSELF TURNED OUT TO BE INSUFFICIENT, and only by accident.
  Smoke-testing produced 0 events in 150s, which is plausible for BTCUSDT. To
  check, the all-symbol stream was tried: 0 events in 90s, which is not plausible
  for anything. Then `btcusdt@aggTrade`, which fires several times a second: also
  0. Diagnosis:

    wss://fstream.binance.com/...    connects, state=1, sends NOTHING, any stream
    wss://stream.binance.com:9443/.. works fine, same machine, same second
    https://fapi.binance.com REST    works fine (the 21.x collector uses it)

  So the futures websocket accepts the connection from this network and then
  stays silent. Nothing raises, nothing retries, the socket looks healthy.

  A process heartbeat would have recorded "alive, zero liquidations" every
  minute, forever, and every one of those hours would have entered the training
  set as a confident zero. The file written to prevent exactly that failure
  contained exactly that failure.

  Fix: subscribe to markPrice@1s alongside forceOrder purely as a canary. The
  exchange sends it once a second unconditionally, so a heartbeat is written only
  while the canary is fresh. "Covered" now means the exchange was demonstrably
  talking to us, not that this process was running.

  A second, subtler version of the same bug survived the first fix: the canary
  was seeded with the current time on connect, as a grace period, so one
  heartbeat was written before anything had been proven — and the run duly
  reported "1 covered hour" against a dead socket. The seed is gone; an open
  socket is not evidence.

23.3 Verification status — honest

  The collector CANNOT be verified end-to-end from here, because the futures
  websocket delivers nothing to this machine. What is verified:

    * the canary correctly refuses to claim coverage against the dead socket
      (observed: warning printed, no heartbeat file, no CSV rows);
    * parse_event against Binance's documented payload, including the side
      convention (forced SELL = a LONG liquidated), which is invertible without
      changing any magnitude and so is asserted rather than eyeballed;
    * zero-vs-missing in the aggregate, both directions.

    6 offline tests, data_fetch/test_liquidations.py.

  What is NOT verified: that a real forceOrder event round-trips. That needs a
  network where fstream responds. First deploy should check `--status` shows a
  live heartbeat within a few minutes, and events accumulating within a day.

23.4 Also fixed while here

  aggregate() now treats an hour containing a received event as covered even if
  no heartbeat landed in it — receiving an event IS proof of liveness, and
  without this an event arriving before the session's first beat was discarded.

23.5 State of the data-collection bet

    derivatives (REST, 30d retention)   collecting since 2026-09-09, 1.0 months
    liquidations (websocket, no history) code ready, unverified, awaiting deploy
    book depth                           not built — snapshots only, also a daemon

  Neither is modellable for a long time; see 21.5. The point of both is that the
  clock cannot be started retroactively.

24. ONE-SIDED TOUCH — the decomposition that finally beats direction (2026-09-10)
================================================================================

Section 20 established that "will EITHER barrier be touched" is strongly
predictable (AUC 0.81) while "WHICH barrier first" is not (0.55). Those are the
two ends of a spectrum and the middle had never been tried:

    which first      direction        AUC 0.5516   exhausted
    either touched   volatility       AUC 0.8054   strong, not directional
    ONE side touched   ?              untested

"Will the UPPER barrier be touched at all within H bars" is mostly a volatility
question — but the ASYMMETRY between the two sides is directional, and it is a
different estimator of direction than the which-first label.

24.1 The labels

  features.barrier_touch_flags() scans the whole holding window for both
  barriers instead of stopping at the first, so an anchor can touch both, either
  or neither. Over the full frame at 2.5 sigma / 24 bars:

    upper touched 55.68%   lower touched 55.55%   both 22.29%   neither 11.06%

  Consistency checked against the first-touch labels: every label=UP anchor did
  touch the upper barrier, every label=DOWN touched the lower. Alignment checked
  the way that has now caught three bugs — labels[bar] == y, 0 mismatches of
  25416 on both sides, every anchor observable.

24.2 Each side, 36 rolling origins

    touch_upper   per-month AUC 0.6156   33/36 months > 0.5
    touch_lower   per-month AUC 0.6441   36/36 months > 0.5

24.3 THE RESULT — the asymmetry predicts direction better than the direction model

  Scored against the SAME direction labels, on the same anchors, same months:

    predictor                          pooled    per-month   months>0.5
    direction model (incumbent)        0.5358      0.5516       28/36
    p_up alone                         0.5323      0.5335       28/36
    logit(p_up) - logit(p_down)        0.5709      0.5703       32/36
    direction model + 0.5*asymmetry    0.5666      0.5702       31/36

    asymmetry - incumbent, paired per month:
        +0.0187   95% CI [+0.0049, +0.0326]   p = 0.0081   24/36 months better

  The combination does not beat the asymmetry alone (0.5702 vs 0.5703), so the
  asymmetry SUBSUMES the incumbent rather than complementing it. Correlation
  between the two logits is +0.503 — related, not redundant.

24.4 AND IT SURVIVES COSTS, which nothing else has

  Sequential (one position at a time), 8 bp round trip, resolve-rate haircut
  applied — the same pricing that killed every previous candidate:

    coverage  trades    acc    gross/trade   net/entered      t    months+
        2%      121   61.98%    +0.3233%      +0.2058%      2.62     6/8
        5%      279   60.93%    +0.3060%      +0.1905%      4.04    14/18
       10%      515   61.55%    +0.2913%      +0.1775%      5.38    24/35
       20%      975   57.13%    +0.1752%      +0.0749%      4.51    23/36
       50%     2167   55.33%    +0.1286%      +0.0337%      4.91    18/36
      100%     3616   53.46%    +0.0831%      -0.0065%      4.19    16/36

    incumbent for comparison:
       10%      466   53.00%    +0.0754%      -0.0133%      1.32     9/20
      100%     3616   50.94%    +0.0138%      -0.0678%      0.69    12/36

  Net positive at every coverage up to 50%, with t = 4-5 against the incumbent's
  0.7-1.3. Plan 17 concluded "not shown to make money"; this is the first
  variant where that sentence would be wrong.

24.5 Why this should work, stated so it can be attacked

  The which-first label is conditioned on resolution and discards the 11% of
  anchors where neither barrier is hit. Each one-sided label is a cleaner binary
  event defined on every observable anchor, so both models train on ~11% more
  data against a better-posed target, and the difference of two well-estimated
  quantities beats one poorly-estimated one. That is an ordinary decomposition
  argument, not a new signal — the information was always there, asked for badly.

24.6 What would have to be true for this to be wrong

  Stated plainly because the result is large and this programme has produced
  three false positives already (14.3 prior shift, 15 label_autocorr, 18.4
  barrier geometry), every one of which looked like this at first:

    * It is one decomposition, tried once, and it worked. No sweep, so no
      best-of-N inflation — but also no replication.
    * The touch models were never confirmed on the frozen tail the way 20.3
      confirmed the resolution model. That is the obvious next check.
    * The pricing assumes take-profit and stop fills AT the barrier, which is
      optimistic; 17.1 flagged the same assumption.
    * t = 5.38 is computed over 515 SEQUENTIAL trades whose labels still overlap
      somewhat; the block CI has not been computed for this signal yet.

24.7 Next, in order

  1. Frozen-tail confirmation (apps/resolution_backtest, both sides, one fitted
     artifact) — the check that reproduced 20.1 and would catch a harness effect.
  2. Block-bootstrap CI on the net-per-trade figure, as plan 17 does.
  3. Only then: geometry sweep on the asymmetry, and a serving path.
